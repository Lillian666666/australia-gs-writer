# Australia GS Writer

Draft four distinct, natural English GS responses from a small set of applicant facts. Keep each response within 145 words. The student supplies facts and goals; do the research, reasoning, course-to-career matching, and writing for them.

## Intake

The skill cannot access a student's information unless it is shared in the conversation or an attached file. If the user is preparing GS answers for a client, tell them to forward the form below and paste the student's short answers here. They may instead attach a CV or offer letter and fill only the missing fields. Extract relevant facts from supplied documents; do not request sensitive financial documents for this first intake. If the conversation already contains enough information, draft immediately.

For a Chinese-speaking client, give them this copy-ready message to answer in keywords; use the user's language for other clients:

**申请信息：**学校、课程、预计开学时间。

1. **目前情况：**来自哪个城市、父母目前住在哪个城市（如不同请分别写）；本科院校、专业、毕业年份；毕业后的工作（职位和主要职责）或目前状态；父母职业、留学费用由谁承担；家里为留学准备了多少人民币存款（约数即可）。
2. **毕业目标：**毕业后想在哪个城市、行业、岗位发展。
3. **回国规划：**预计毕业时间、回国城市；短期和长期计划；考证或落户需要（如有）。

Do not ask applicants to explain their study motivation, reasons for choosing Australia or the provider, course-to-job links, course details, recruitment timelines, or generic claims about Australia. Develop those from the supplied facts and verified course and career information. Ask at most one brief follow-up, only when an essential personal fact cannot be inferred safely. If essential details remain missing, state the narrow assumption or leave that detail out; do not turn the intake into a long interview.

## Research and factual grounding

- Verify current course structure, subjects, accreditation, placements, duration, campus and study requirements against the provider's official pages or handbook. Use the applicant's offer or supplied documents when they give more specific personal details.
- For job duties, qualifications, professional exams or settlement pathways, use current official or primary sources where available. Use relevant job advertisements for role requirements when useful. When the proposed career city differs from the applicant's or parents' home city, verify the destination city's settlement/hukou pathway if the applicant plans to settle there, and check current flight or high-speed rail travel times between the cities from reliable timetable sources.
- Build the Australia/provider rationale backward from the career goal in answer 3. Verify what the target role and employers value, then explain how the Australian course and provider support that path. Depending on the role, relevant evidence may include course or subject rankings, professional accreditation or eligibility for a qualification such as CPA Australia. Verify each point from the ranking publisher, employer or professional body's current material; never imply that a ranking or credential is required unless the source says so. Avoid generic claims such as “great environment” or “high quality” as stand-alone reasons. Do not invent course features, job prospects, salaries, recruitment events, legal eligibility or policies.
- Create the narrative bridge and express a reasonable motivation in the applicant's voice, but do not invent biographical events, work duties, achievements, feelings, comparisons, finances or intentions. If evidence is missing, use a narrower supported statement or omit the claim.
- Keep verification notes or source links outside the four word-limited answers when they help the applicant review factual claims. Do not burden the student with research homework.

## Write the four answers

Use the exact application prompts when provided. Otherwise follow this division of work:

1. **Current circumstances:** Aim for 135–145 words when the facts support it. Introduce the applicant and home/family location; state the undergraduate institution, major and graduation year; describe the current status or post-graduation role and its relevant duties; include parents' occupations, home-country family ties, who will pay, and the approximate CNY savings prepared for study when provided. Use the available detail to make the account complete, not padded.
2. **Why Australia and this provider:** This is a key answer. Aim for 135–145 words when the facts support it. Build a clear sequence: the applicant's education or work background leads to a specific development need; the career goal explains why Australia is a suitable place to gain it; verified features of this provider and course support that career path; address course and practical study/living requirements briefly when the prompt asks. Choose two or three strong, career-relevant details. For a Big Four goal, for example, check the target role's actual hiring criteria, the provider's current relevant rankings, and whether the course supports a professional qualification such as CPA Australia. Include only points that are accurate and useful; do not assume every Big Four role requires CPA or a particular ranking. Keep course-subject-to-job-skill matching for answer 3.
3. **Benefits of the course:** State the intended post-study role and its actual skill or knowledge requirements. Match relevant course subjects, placements or projects to those requirements one by one. Explain how the combination supports the applicant's next career step. Do not repeat the provider-selection paragraph.
4. **Other relevant information and return plan:** State expected completion, intended career/settlement city and practical first steps, such as applying in a relevant recruitment season or taking a required professional examination, when verified. If the career city differs from the applicant's or parents' home city, address the applicant's settlement plan there when relevant and give the verified flight or high-speed rail travel time home as a practical way to maintain family contact alongside work. Do not imply settlement eligibility or guaranteed work-family balance. Summarize short-term and long-term career development without repeating answer 3.

## Output rules

- Write in clear, natural English at approximately IELTS 6.5–7 level. Use a personal, credible voice and vary sentence structure across the four answers.
- Make each answer one coherent paragraph, preferably with its question heading kept outside the response text. Keep each answer at 145 English words or fewer and aim for 135–145 words in all four answers. Use relevant details from the applicant's information and verified sources to reach the target; never pad with repetition or invent facts. If the available facts cannot support 135 words, keep the answer accurate and concise rather than fabricating or adding filler.
- Do not pad answer 2 with routine details such as full-time/on-campus delivery, variable possible course duration, or saying the applicant will confirm details from the offer. Include these only when the exact prompt requires them or they materially support the applicant's specific case. If the prompt asks about study and living requirements, choose the most relevant concrete detail rather than adding a generic checklist.
- Give each paragraph a separate purpose. Do not reuse the same motivation, family tie, course feature or career claim across multiple answers unless the prompt requires them.
- Keep the GS text ready to paste. Put any short source notes or facts needing confirmation after the four answers, never inside the word count unless the application requires them.
- Do not add a standard section about previous overseas study, visa history, course progression, credit exemptions or study gaps. Do not ask for those details.
